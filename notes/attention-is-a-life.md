# Attention Is a Life

We often imagine life as a collection of big decisions. In practice, it is also the quiet sum of what receives our attention each day.

Attention is not infinite. Every hour spent somewhere is an hour that cannot be spent elsewhere. This is not a reason for guilt; it is a reason to choose with care.

Distraction is not always laziness. Sometimes it is a refuge from uncertainty, boredom, or the discomfort of beginning. Naming the discomfort can be more useful than insulting ourselves for feeling it.

A good day does not need to be spectacular. It can be a day in which we gave one important thing an honest stretch of attention, noticed what pulled us away, and returned without drama.

The goal is not perfect focus. The goal is to become a little more deliberate about what gets our limited time.

**Reflection:** What did my attention serve today—and was that close to what I value?
